Design direction B — VALUE LADDER. Abandon the two-column grid. The six paid tiers become one single
column that physically GROWS as it descends, so a player feels the escalation in the size of the paper

  reads as quiet utility, not as the main event.
- The words "ACORN POUCHES" sit on a thin ink hairline divider that spans the screen, broken at its center

- Below the divider, the six tiers are six full-width horizontal paper plates in one column, stacked with a
  small gutter. Their heights increase monotonically: tier 1 is a slim shallow strip, and each successive
  plate is taller than the last, so tier 6 is roughly two and a half times the height of tier 1. Their

- Each plate has the same internal reading order left to right: hero container, then the amount in large
  ink, then the price in an action-green paper button at the right end. Because the plates grow, the
  numerals and buttons on later plates are genuinely larger, not merely spaced further apart.
- Their surface value also climbs: the lower tiers are quiet pale-meadow paper, the middle tiers warm cream,
  and tier 6 is the richest — a warm-cream face over a reward-gold under-sheet whose thin gold rim shows all
  the way around it, with the gold BEST VALUE seal at its upper left corner.
- A slender vertical cut-paper measuring spine runs down the left margin beside the column: a narrow cream
  ribbon with one small notch aligned to each tier's center, and the ribbon itself widens slightly with each
  notch as it descends. It is the visual proof of the ladder and carries no text.
- The coral POPULAR ribbon crosses the upper-left corner of the tier-3 plate.
