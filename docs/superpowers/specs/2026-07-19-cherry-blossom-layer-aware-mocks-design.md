# Cherry Blossom Layer-Aware Mocks Design

## Goal

Create three dressed reference mocks for a cherry-blossom temple garden that preserve the approved paper-cut grain language while making the later three-family production workflow practical:

1. stable backdrop;
2. four replaceable hero-object groups;
3. fixed covering vegetation and stone clusters.

The mocks are composition references, not runtime layers. They are intentionally authored so the production layers can be generated from the approved mock without deconstructing dozens of small elements.

## Pipeline

- `map_mode`: `scene_mode`
- `visual_model`: `layered_raster`
- `runtime_object_model`: `separate_props + foreground_occluders`
- `collision_model`: `none` for this concept checkpoint
- `engine_target`: project-native concept reference, later Scene Workbench reconstruction
- generation path: three independent built-in image-generation calls using local image references

## Reference roles

- `cherry_blossom_garden_temple_concept_v1.png` is the primary composition, object-scale, temple-content, and portrait-rhythm reference.
- `cherry_blossom_garden_original_mock_v1.png` is the secondary material, palette, camera, lighting, and paper-cut-grain reference.

Both are references, not edit targets. The three results are new compositions.

## Shared layer-aware contract

Each mock contains exactly four customizable hero groups:

1. one modest tea pavilion or prayer pavilion;
2. one open temizuya purification pavilion containing its stone basin, bamboo spout, running water, and ladles;
3. one vermilion torii gate;
4. one connected pond precinct containing the pond, koi, lily pads, and one small arched bridge.

Each group occupies a distinct visual socket. Hero groups do not touch one another and do not share foliage. Their important silhouettes remain visible. Fixed cover vegetation may overlap only the bottom 5-10 percent of a structure or the outer 5-10 percent of a pond bank, enough to blend the contact without hiding doors, decks, taps, basin rims, gate openings, roofs, bridge deck, or koi.

The stable backdrop is one continuous pale-ivory zen-sand garden with broad subtle rake marks, a permanent stepping-stone path, low ground markings, distant mountain bands, and simple horizon planting. The dressed reference may show a paper sky and separate cloud shapes, but no sky or cloud is assumed to belong to the later runtime foundation.

The fixed covering family uses a few broad sakura canopies at the page edges plus medium bush-and-rock masses at hero contacts. Avoid scattered individual flowers, lanterns, statues, fences, bonsai pots, signs, benches, extra buildings, extra water features, loose ornaments, and debris.

## Variations

### A - Faithful axial approach

Closest to the temple concept: pavilion upper-right, pond upper-left/middle, temizuya near center, and torii in the lower approach. A clear stepping-stone path links the gate, purification station, and pavilion.

### B - Gentle S-curve garden

Preserve the same scale and object vocabulary but let the path sweep in a readable S-curve. Use asymmetry and negative sand space while keeping every hero socket isolated and easy to measure.

### C - Terraced diagonal garden

Use a shallow upper terrace and a diagonal route across the page. The composition may be more inventive, but it must remain recognizably related to the approved temple concept and honor the same four sockets.

## Visual contract

- portrait output normalized to exactly 930 x 1691 pixels;
- elevated three-quarter top-down camera;
- matte layered cardstock, tactile cut edges, fine fibrous paper tooth, lightly mottled printed pigment, and short soft down-right contact shadows;
- warm ivory sand, muted blue-gray mountains and roof tiles, vermilion structures, deep garden greens, coral-pink blossoms, and restrained turquoise water;
- a few broad readable masses rather than dense ornament;
- no text, labels, numerals, UI, logos, borders, or watermarks;
- no photorealism, glossy 3D plastic, clay, sticker outlines, watercolor, gouache, brushwork, black outlines, or cinematic darkness.

## Acceptance

Each mock must show all four hero groups once and only once, preserve an uninterrupted gate opening and path, keep the temizuya's water hardware legible, show the bridge as part of the pond precinct, and leave enough unobstructed silhouette for later one-by-one hero generation. The three images should feel like one scene family while offering meaningful compositional choice.

Final approval remains visual review by the user.
